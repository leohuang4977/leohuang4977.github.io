What I do

  • Experiments & causal inference

    I design studies that can separate cause from coincidence. At the University of Toronto I ran an 8-week randomized controlled trial with more than 500 people, and on product teams I've used A/B and comparative designs to let behavior settle design arguments.

    EvidenceAccordion navigation testMortgage switching

  • Decision & journey design

    I look for the points where people hesitate or give up, then help redesign the steps around them. At a major Canadian bank I co-developed the wireframes and prototypes for a Contact Us redesign, and user satisfaction rose 20% after it.

  • Human–AI interaction

    My PhD research asks what sustained use of social chatbots does to people's wellbeing and relationships. In industry I've studied how people take up AI features and agents at work, and what it takes for them to trust one.

About

I've spent my whole research life circling one question: how do people actually work? I've chased it through the body, the brain and the social worlds people build around themselves, and now into what changes when AI becomes part of those worlds. The field kept changing. The question never did.

My research at the University of Toronto looks at what happens to people's wellbeing and real relationships when they use social chatbots over time. I study it with longitudinal and cross-sectional designs, including an 8-week randomized trial with more than 500 people. My PhD defence is planned for December 2026.

In industry I've taken the same methods to product and business decisions, like why customers switch mortgage providers and how people take up AI at work. I usually stay involved past the readout, in the design reviews where findings turn into a redesigned flow or a scoped pilot.
